Greedy Pair-Merge: Background Theory

Bottom-up agglomerative clustering and graph coarsening

  • The simplest coarsening engine that still feels like CAD: every node starts as its own cluster.
  • Used in electronic CAD, mesh coarsening, and large-graph partitioning.
  • Purely greedy: once two clusters merge, there is no undo or backtracking.
  • Each iteration picks only the locally best legal pair — local optimum ≠ global optimum.

We repeatedly merge the most attractive legal pair, update the graph, and stop when we reach target cluster count K.

Engine Rules & Specification

Initialization, contraction, termination, cutsize

RuleSpecification
InitializationStart from singletons: every cell is its own individual cluster.
ContractionRepeatedly contract the highest-affinity legal pair into a supernode, then update the graph.
TerminationStop at target K, or when no legal positive-weight pair remains.
Cutsize reportingReport the final partition and cutsize: the sum of edge weights crossing cluster boundaries.

No fake merges. If no valid pair exists, the engine must stop — it will not invent a merge just to hit K.

AffinitySum weightsCurrent edge weights between two clusters; parallel connections add (AC 1 + BC 4 → 5).
IllegalSkip pairAffinity ≤ 0, or combined size |A| + |B| exceeds the capacity limit.
PickMaximum affinityAmong remaining legal pairs, take the one with the largest affinity.
Tie-breakLexicographic labelsSort member names in each cluster and join them; pick the lexicographically smaller label (A < D, AB < D).

On tiny_graph, A–B and D–E both have weight 5; A–B wins because A < D. After that, AB–C ties D–E at 5; AB < D so C is absorbed first.

Critical Implementation Pitfalls

What breaks goldens if the engine is slightly wrong

Myopic decisions

Greedy selection has no undo. An attractive first merge can lock the solver into a suboptimal global cut.

Capacity validation order

Check capacity constraints before ranking pairs, never after you have already picked a winner.

Affinity updates

After contraction, recompute and sum parallel edge weights accurately, or later clusters go silently wrong.

Deterministic tie-breaking

Enforce lexicographic sorting on combined labels so AB–C vs D–E is stable and lab tests do not flake.

If capacity blocks every remaining pair, stop. Do not invent a merge just to force target K.

Summary: Greedy Pair-Merge

Key takeaways for CAD & graph partitioning

  • Fast bottom-up coarsening engine for CAD and mesh problems.
  • Greedy limitation: no backtracking, so a local optimum may not equal the global optimum.
  • Capacity constraints may prevent reaching target cluster count K — do not invent merges.
  • Correct affinity calculation and a stable lex tie-break are required for reproducible results.
