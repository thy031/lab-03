Greedy Pair-Merge: Background Theory

Bottom-up agglomerative graph coarsening

  • Every node starts as its own individual cluster.
  • Purely greedy: no undo or backtracking once merged.
  • Selects the locally optimal legal pair in every iteration.
  • Widely used in CAD, mesh coarsening, and partitioning.

Local optimal choices do not guarantee a globally optimal partition. We repeatedly merge the most attractive legal pair, update the graph, and stop at target cluster count K.

Engine Rules & Selection Mechanics

Pair ranking, affinity & termination

SignalRule
InitializationSingletons — every cell is its own cluster
ContractionHighest-affinity legal pair merges into a supernode
TerminationStop at K, or when no legal positive-weight pair remains
AffinitySum of current edge weights between two clusters
IllegalSkip if affinity ≤ 0, or if size exceeds capacity
Tie-breakLexicographic cluster labels (sorted member names)

The algorithm will not invent fake merges to hit K if no valid pairs exist. After each merge, sort remaining cluster labels so later ties stay stable.

On tiny_graph, A–B vs D–E are both weight 5; A–B wins because A < D. After that, AB–C vs D–E are both 5; AB < D so C is absorbed first.

Summary: Greedy Pair-Merge

Key takeaways for CAD & graph partitioning

  • Fast bottom-up coarsening engine for CAD and mesh problems.
  • Strict greedy limitation: no backtracking (local ≠ global optimum).
  • Capacity limits can prevent reaching target cluster count K.
  • Correct affinity updates and stable tie-breaks are required.
